# KLAPKA production standard, v1

Approved by Koks on 2026-11-08 after the Sony Ericsson W995 ("looks exactly as it should").
Every phone goes through these steps. Changing the standard needs Koks's yes; experiments are fine,
but they stay experiments until he approves them.

**Principle:** geometry comes from data, appearance comes from a photo, printed text comes from data.

## Deliverables per phone (`phones/<id>/`)

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `phone.json` | the phone's data ([docs/FORMAT.md](FORMAT.md)) |
| `build.json` | how it was made: template, photo and licence, seed scores, text fixes, acceptance |
| `body.png` | master: RGBA, 12 px/mm, 1 mm transparent padding, screen not cut |
| `skin.png` | the master with the screen hole at the exact native aspect |
| `mi-band-192x490.png` | Mi Band 7/8/9 export, transparent around the phone and in the screen |
| `preview.png`, `thumb.png` | page preview (with a sample clock) and gallery thumbnail |
| `README.md` | the phone's page, generated by `klapka.py pages` |
| `work/` | not committed: template, renders with sidecar JSONs (prompt, seed, size, model), crops, sheets |

## Rules

0. **Which phones belong here (Koks, 2026-11-11): only phones with a keypad of number keys below
   the screen** in the view that is shown. A keyboard whose middle keys carry the digits in a keypad
   layout counts (Sony Ericsson P1i, M600i). Touchscreen-only phones are out, so are phones with a full
   QWERTY keyboard instead of a keypad (Sony Ericsson txt, Aspen), and so are phones whose keyboard
   slides out sideways and turns the phone landscape (Sony Ericsson Xperia X1, X2, X10 mini pro, mini
   pro, pro and PLAY, txt pro, Vivaz pro).
1. **Sliders, flips and swivels are always shown open** (keypad slid out, clamshell unfolded, swivel
   turned out; swivels added by Koks, 2026-11-09): `view: "open"`, `size_mm` is the open size,
   `layout_mm.seams` marks where the parts meet.
   **Exception (Koks, 2026-11-08): phones whose keypad sits on a flip that closes over the
   touchscreen** (Sony Ericsson P800, P900, P910i, P990i) are shown **closed**, because that is how
   the keypad was seen: `view: "closed"`, `size_mm` is the closed size, the screen is the part of the
   display visible above the closed flip (`display.native_px` = that visible area), and
   `layout_mm.seams` marks the flip's edges.
2. **Reference photos** follow `docs/REFERENCES.md` (factory configuration first). Any reasonable
   angle works; nothing has to be frontal. Photos are input only: never published, source and licence
   recorded in `phone.json`.
3. **The screen is drawn at the exact native aspect**, its size from the diagonal; the final hole has
   the exact native aspect too.
4. **Images come from Qwen-Image 2.1 on a stable-diffusion.cpp server** (the maintainer uses KLIF
   System CGI, preset `qwen-image-21`, Turbo INT8): 8 steps, cfg 1.0, euler, one request at a time.
   `tools/backend.py` finds the server.
5. **Canvas sizes are multiples of 32 and at most 1,048,576 px** (704×1408 for ~0.45 phones,
   640×1632 for long sliders). Larger canvases slow sd.cpp down and did not fix text.
6. **Printed text from pass 1 is never trusted.** Every text and key label is read letter by letter;
   anything wrong goes through the text pass (step 5).
7. **Prompts come only from the standard IDs in `tools/prompts.json`**; experiments live in
   `tools/prompts_archive.json`.
9. **Text fixes are minimal:** redraw only what is wrong. Redraws can drift the small icons next to
   key labels, so a correct row is never redrawn.
8. **Prototype phase:** no test suites. Acceptance = the numbers below + Koks's visual approval.

## Steps

`tools/klapka.py` runs each step; the scripts it calls are listed for reference. The runbook with
what to look at after each step is [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md).

| # | Command | What happens | Underlying tool |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | `new <id> --brand B --model M`, fill `phone.json`, `measure <id> PHOTO` | data: real size (open size for sliders and flips, measured if no spec gives it), display diagonal and resolution, screen top edge, keypad block, seams, texts, keys, sources | – |
| 1 | `template <id>` | layout drawn to scale on the largest canvas that fits (multiples of 32, ≤ 1 MP, phone ~92% of the height); the screen at the exact native aspect | `make_template.py` |
| 2 | `photo <id> PATH --source URL --license L --author A` | appearance photo, EXIF-corrected, longest side 1024 | – |
| 3 | `pass1 <id>` | seeds 1–4: template first, photo second, prompt `koks_template`; ranked relative to the phone's own box: a seed passes with a white background, body aspect within 3% and the screen centred within 1%; among passing seeds the best screen match and left/right symmetry win; overlay sheet | `cgi_edit.py`, `score_render.py` |
| 4 | `pass2 <id>` | background removal on the best seed (prompt `cutout`); moves nothing | `cgi_edit.py` |
| 5 | `grid <id>`, `rows <id>`, `textfix <id> --row N` / `--keypad` / `--text T --box …` | every printed text read; only the wrong areas (a row of keys, preferred; the whole keypad only when most rows are wrong; a word) cut out, enlarged to an exact multiple of 32, redrawn with the exact wording (`fix_key_row` / `fix_keypad_tpl` / `fix_text`), pasted back colour-matched, alpha untouched | `fix_text.py`, `cgi_edit.py` |
| 6 | `finish <id>` | master `body.png` (12 px/mm), screen hole at the exact native aspect (`skin.png`), Mi Band export, preview, thumbnail, measured acceptance | `clean_alpha.py`, `mark_screen.py` |
| 7 | `mark <id> --text-ok` (and `--approved` by the maintainer) | the visual checks recorded in `build.json` | – |
| 8 | `pages` | phone pages; the catalog (`catalog/`: by year, by brand, by type, all phones); the front page (newest additions, browse links); `catalog.json` | – |
| – | `header` (maintainer) | the README header animation from the hand-picked line-up in `assets/header.json` | `make_header.py` |

If no seed passes (white background, aspect within 3%), run `pass1 <id> --seeds 1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8`.
To restart the text fixes from the clean cutout: `pass2 <id> --reuse`.

## Acceptance

Pass/fail (`finish` prints `PASS` only when all of these hold):

| Check | Threshold |
|---|---|
| body aspect vs spec | within ±3% (Koks, 2026-11-08: renders land 2–4% off; ≤3% is acceptable). A wider limit for one phone only with the maintainer's approval, recorded in its `build.json` under `acceptance_exceptions` with the reason |
| lean (mean angle of the two sides, after the automatic straightening) | ≤ 0.3° |
| screen hole aspect vs native | error ≤ 0.2% |
| display window on the body's axis | the window's centre ≤ 1.3% of the body width from the axis (rows with side buttons ignored) |
| display window size | 0.9–1.4× the spec screen size; an automatic window also needs edge strength ≥ 4 on every side |
| visible screen border | on the finished skin, the dark band from the hole to the bezel differs by ≤ 0.3 mm between opposite sides (left/right, top/bottom). The hole is centred in the window (the inside edge of the bezel); if a dark LCD border outside the window makes one side wider, the hole is re-centred in the whole dark area. On a dark front (band more than 2 mm past the window) only the window counts. The window is confirmed visually on `work/screen_check.png` (`mark --screen-ok`) |
| printed text | every letter right, icons plausible (`mark --text-ok`) |
| text-pass seams | none visible at 2× |
| maintainer | visual approval (`mark --approved`) |

Measured and recorded in `build.json`, for information only (Koks, 2026-11-08): taper (sides
converging), side tilt, edge jitter and left/right symmetry; also the hole's margins inside the
window (the visible border above is what the eye judges). Rounded or tapering sides and buttons on
one side are part of the design, so these are not pass/fail.

## Tried and rejected (do not retry without a new idea)

| Approach | Why it lost |
|---|---|
| Cut the background out of the photo | keeps perspective, noise, wear and soft labels; 1 px brown rim from the old background |
| Qwen "re-render" / "front view" prompts on the photo alone | the edit keeps the photo's layout: perspective and visible sides stay |
| Perspective rectification (4 corners + homography) | fixes tilt and keystone, not a visible side face |
| Second reference image with a sharp keypad | helps only when such a photo exists; not general |
| 2 MP canvases (1024×2048) | sharper letters, digits fixed, letters still wrong; ~3 min per image |
| Labels drawn into the template | partly right, and one render copied the flat template, ignoring the photo |

## What the first batch taught (2026-11-08, seven Sony Ericssons)

- The render keeps a little of the photo's viewing angle: an angled photo gave a K700i with one side
  panel showing and the screen off-centre. Seeds are now also ranked by screen centring and mirror
  symmetry, and frontal photos are preferred.
- Lean is fixed automatically in `finish` (T610 0.35°, K800i 0.39° before; ~0° after).
- Key rows must be found on the render, not taken from the template: template rows landed on the
  wrong keys and duplicated rows.
- Redrawing a whole keypad fixes letters but drifts icons; fix rows, `restore` the right ones.
- A wrong glyph tends to survive repeated text fixes; another passing seed is the fix.
- Sister models share a chassis, not necessarily a keypad position (K750i vs W800i).
- Spec sheets can be wrong (T610 diagonal 1.9″ does not fit its glass; 1.8″ does).
- The automatic screen hole can miss a display that shows content; the overlay must be checked, and
  `--screen` gives it by hand.
- Rounded or tapering sides and one-sided buttons are design, so taper, edge jitter and symmetry are
  recorded but not pass/fail (Koks).
- A photo held at an angle can leave the keypad rows tilted even after perspective correction (P1i,
  1.5–2°): the render copies it. Prefer an orthographic press render or a straight-on photo, and
  check the rows against level lines before finishing. An automatic tilt measure was tried and was
  not reliable enough (keypads with arched rows, icons), so this stays a visual check.
- Pixels the model left semi-transparent inside the body carry the matte colour (magenta/purple);
  `clean_alpha` now refills them from the nearest opaque pixel (P1i side button).

## What the K batch taught (2026-11-08)

- Fixing a whole row while `key_icons` lists icons for several keys can scatter icons onto the wrong
  keys (an envelope on 0, a lock on 2). Fix only the wrong key, describing that key alone.
- `--text` alone uses the word prompt and renders a description literally ("1' with a small
  envelope…" printed on the key). Single keys go through the row prompt with `--row N --box … --text`.
- A tiny press image (under ~300 px tall) makes the model invent details (a "SONY" badge, doubled
  digits); a sharper photo of the same phone fixes it, even a cropped one, since the template sets the
  geometry.

- Screen holes can land off the display: snapped to the outer frame on one side and the picture on the
  other (K310i: low and to the right), or to a weak edge when the render moved the display (K300i: too
  low). `finish` now checks the hole's centre against the body's axis and writes `work/screen_check.png`;
  a person or agent must confirm it (`--screen-ok`). `--snap combo` and `--screen` fix misses.

- Keystone (photo taken from above): the K600i photo was 16% wider at the bottom than the top; the
  render came out 6% too wide. Rectifying the four corners first fixed it.
- On phones whose display shows dark content, the edge finder can stop on an inner UI line (K530i,
  K770i): the screen check catches it; give the lit display with `--screen`.

- Screen holes must sit centred in the display window, not on the rendered picture: renders place the
  picture off-centre in the glass. `finish` now finds the window (inside edge of the bezel), paints it
  all as dark glass and centres the hole in it. The automatic window finder is unreliable (it caught
  the bezel ring or the screen content on most early phones), so the window is checked on
  `screen_check.png` and usually given by hand with `--window`.

## What the end of the K series taught (2026-11-09)

- Snapping a hand-given window to the nearest strong edge (±7 px) can take the outer edge of a thin
  dark LCD border on one side and the inner edge on the other: holes 0.3–0.7 mm off (K220i up and
  left, K530i, K770i) while every number passed. Edges are read off luminance profiles across each
  side and given exactly (`--no-refine`); the visible-border check now measures what the eye sees.
- A black front (K330) makes the visible border run into the body; re-centring on it moved the hole
  4 mm. It is skipped when the band runs more than 2 mm past the window.
- Word fixes on small key labels often return the same garbled letters (K330 "AGC", K220i "DBŁ" six
  times). With most rows wrong, `--keypad` got every label right at the first seed on K220i, K330 and
  K630i; the few leftovers then took one word fix each.
- A word box that cuts a glyph erases the part inside it (K200i `*`, K630i `5`); `restore` puts it back.

## What the first W batch taught (2026-11-09)

- On phones with one glass panel over the display and the face (W880i, W890i, W910i) the brand,
  or the Walkman logo, is printed on that glass. The automatic window takes the whole panel and would
  paint the print black: the window is the LCD rectangle inside the panel, read off a profile.
- A cutout photo can still carry perspective (W580i: top narrower than the bottom). Every render came
  out 3–7% short. Rectifying the photo fixed the size, but the phone still looked crooked (Koks);
  a straight-on press render of the same model fixed it. A press render beats a corrected photo.
- A seed's keypad redraw can leave the same wrong letters; another seed of the keypad redraw (`textfix
  --keypad --seed 2`, after `pass2 --reuse`) got every label right on the W200i.
- Icons the model keeps redrawing wrong (W200i `#`) and specks copied from a photo (W610i) are
  removed by inpainting or neighbour interpolation, recorded in `build.json` `text_fixes` as
  `inpaint`; no image model is involved. A missing icon is better than a wrong one.
- Operator logos on an otherwise good photo (W910i "orange") are painted out on a copy of the photo
  before pass 1.

## What the second W batch taught (2026-11-09)

- Straight-on press renders of open sliders, swivels and flips exist on gsm.magazyn.pl and
  newmobile.com (`docs/REFERENCES.md`); a press render beats any photo held at an angle.
- Press renders often carry a mirror reflection under the phone: measure the body above it.
- A dark phone on a dark press render loses its light edges to a dark-pixel threshold (W900i measured
  171.6 mm, really 166.4 mm). Check the open length on a second render or on the finished render.
- A render can copy the template's outline around the phone (W850i seed 2). The seed score then
  measures the outline, not the phone, and a 5% too narrow phone passes. Look at the winner; measure
  the phone itself when an outline shows.
- The template is a plain rectangle: a flip whose top half is narrower than the bottom (W300i) scores
  6-10% "too narrow" on every seed. Judge such a phone against the photo, not the score.
- A phone whose screen really sits off the body axis (W850i: 1.5% on the photo, 1.7% on the render)
  needs a maintainer exception for `window_centre_x_pct`, recorded like the aspect exception.

## What the third W batch taught (2026-11-09)

- Small press images (about 140 x 400 px) give soft renders with invented labels; the whole keypad
  then needs a redraw. A larger press render of the same model is worth asking for (W705, W760i).
- A blurred logo (the Walkman "W." symbol) is redrawn sharp with the row prompt and a description of
  the logo (`textfix --row 1 --box ... --text "the Sony WALKMAN logo ..."`); the word prompt only
  rewrites the letters.
- A replacement-housing render shows the glass opening, not the display: on the W760i the opening
  also covers the Walkman print below the LCD, 2 mm lower than the lit screen. Measure the screen on
  a render with the display lit.
- When the template's keypad block misses the real keys, a keypad redraw writes a second row of
  labels (W350i, W980i). Measure the block on the render, fix `layout_mm.keypad`, and redraw.

## What the last W batch taught (2026-11-09)

- The cutout can turn a dark body semi-transparent and tint it (W715, seed 1: alpha 205-245 over a
  quarter of the screen); `clean_alpha` then refills those pixels and smears the face. Check that the
  body inside the silhouette is opaque after pass 2; another cutout seed fixes it.
- Word-fix boxes must hold only the letters, read off the clean cutout's bright pixels. Boxes read
  off a coarse grid overlapped the digit or icon next to them, and the new text was pasted beside
  the old one (W960i, W100, W715).
- Two models with the same chassis but a different colour scheme get their own skins (W705 silver,
  W715 black); a cosmetic twin with the same look is described on one skin (Koks).
- `clean_alpha` used to recolour every interior pixel below alpha 250 from its nearest opaque
  neighbour (meant for the matte showing through, P1i). Chrome side rims sit at alpha 210-250, so
  they came out blotchy (W715, and milder on about 30 phones; Koks spotted it). Now only nearly
  transparent or matte-coloured pixels are refilled; the rims of the affected phones were rebuilt
  with the fixed code, their screen holes untouched.

## What the first T batch taught (2026-11-09)

- Spec sheets of 2002-2003 phones disagree (T200: 106 x 46 mm on GSMArena, 105 x 48 mm on teltarif).
  The press render's proportions decide which one to take.
- A photo of a worn unit with an operator logo drew the wrong brand style (T68i: serif "SONY
  ERICSSON" from a later cover, 7% too narrow). A press render of the original gave the right
  logo and the spec width; when a skin looks off, look for a press render first (Koks).
- A small panel (101 x 80, 1.6-1.7 in) sits in a deep black frame; measure the window to the
  frame's outer edge, or the screen looks too small for its diagonal. (First measured too short
  on T300, which looked like non-square pixels; it was not.)
- Several keys came out with the same wrong glyph after every word fix (T68i PORS, T300 ARS, a ringed
  C). A letter edit by hand (a Q tail, a C lettered in the key's ink) is cleaner than more
  seeds; every such edit is recorded in `build.json` `text_fixes`.

## What the second T batch taught (2026-11-10)

- `--keypad` again got every label right at the first seed on four phones (T230, T290i, T606, T206,
  including labels before the digit and `0 NEXT`). It can also turn an icon that was right (a
  voicemail tape, a space symbol) into nonsense: put those back from the clean cutout (`restore
  --rows 1`, or a box around the symbol).
- A single-key fix can redraw the digit in another weight (T270i: a bold `*` among thin digits);
  restore the digit from the cutout and keep the new letters.
- A word fix on a logo printed on dark glass drew ghost shadows twice (T250i). The cutout had one
  wrong letter (an i drawn as l), which a hand edit fixed: the stroke cut into a dot and a stem at the
  x-height of the letters beside it. A K that came back R-like twice (T270i) was drawn by hand in the
  letter colour. Both are recorded in `build.json` `text_fixes`.
- A small window-limited hole can miss the 0.2% aspect limit through rounding alone (T206 at 1.3in);
  a diagonal 0.01in away rounds cleanly. Note it in `size_note` when no spec gives the diagonal.
- A light silver rim on a white background scores 3-5% narrow on every seed (T250i, 8 seeds); the
  cutout measured 2.5%. Measure the cutout before rendering more seeds.
- Press renders on esato.com are cropped tight; geometry is measured on a full, uncropped render
  (gsm.magazyn.pl) of the same model.

## What the last T batch taught (2026-11-10)

- Press renders with a mirror reflection under the phone (T303, T707, T715) gave seeds that drew the
  reflection too, or copied the flat template; the open length was first measured 15 mm long on the
  T715 (the reflection counted). Crop the reflection off the photo before `photo` and measure the
  body above it.
- Xataka Movil galleries (`xatakamovil.com/galeria/sony-ericsson-<model>`) hold the official Sony
  Ericsson press sets (`front_open` and the like, up to 768 px tall); newmobile.com has the same
  sets, but only at about 130 x 360.
- A keypad block measured on the photo can still miss the rendered keys by a few pixels; measure
  it on the render (or on the cutout) before `--keypad` (T700, T707, T715).
- The `i` of the Sony Ericsson logo often comes out as a tall `l` (four phones in this series); the
  stroke is cut into a dot and a stem at the x-height of `r` and `c` by hand. A model name copied from
  a variant's render (`T280a`) is rewritten with the word prompt.
- A cutout can make small coloured details transparent (the T707's blue speaker dots, alpha 0-200
  with the colour kept); `clean_alpha` would refill them dark. Set their alpha to 255 on the cutout
  first and record it.

## What the C series taught (2026-11-10)

- Slider press renders on gsm.magazyn.pl are cropped at the top and bottom edge (C903, C905): every
  seed came out 3-5% narrow and none passed. The cutout of the best-looking seed measured within the
  limit (C903 -1.4%, C905 -2.7%); measure the cutout before rendering more seeds.
- The open length of the C905 measured on the render (134 mm) matches the only published figure
  (about 135 mm, Trusted Reviews).
- A cutout can turn a whole dark phone semi-transparent (C702, a third of the body at alpha below
  250); another cutout seed (`pass2 --seed 2`) fixed it.
- A keypad redraw writes the `#` key's space symbol as a second, smaller `#` (C702, C903) or as
  invented glyphs (C905). The wrong glyph is painted out; a missing icon is better than a wrong one.
- A single-key redraw can change the key's main glyph (C905: `a*` instead of `*`). The area is put
  back from the keypad redraw's saved crop (`work/fixN_crop.png`, the state before fix N) and the
  rest is fixed by hand.

## What the S series taught (2026-11-10)

- The keypad block in `layout_mm.keypad` was off on five of six phones (read off a small press
  render); a keypad redraw then writes labels between the keys. Measure the block on the render
  (`final.png` with a ruler) before `--keypad`; after that all six keypads came out right at the
  first seed, apart from one key on the S312.
- The spec width can include side buttons (S312: 46 mm with the buttons, 0.454 without them on two
  press renders): every seed then scores 3-6% narrow. Measure the body on the render without the
  buttons.
- A cutout can go semi-transparent over most of the body (S312 84%, S710a 18% on the screen); a
  second cutout seed (`pass2 --seed 2`) fixed both. Checking the cutout's alpha catches it before
  the text fixes.
- No straight-on press render of the open S700i exists; a review photo of the open phone (watermark
  painted out, rotated upright) gave a clean render. The S710a (the same body in black) gives the
  open length a second check.
- A real opening through the body (the S600i's slot in the keypad flap) was filled by `clean_alpha`
  with smeared colour (Koks spotted it). `build.json` `keep_holes: N` keeps transparent openings of at
  least N px (render pixels) through `finish`; the slot's ragged edge was smoothed and its rim
  inpainted from the body around it. `skin-black.png` shows the opening black.
- A black screen glass with a grey gloss reflection on one side (S302) makes the frame around the
  hole uneven. The glass outline is painted black and the brand print kept on it by brightness.

## What the Xperia series taught (2026-11-11)

- Of the seven Xperias with a keyboard only the Pureness qualifies (rule 0): the others slide their
  keyboard out sideways and turn landscape.
- A see-through display (Pureness) has no LCD border: the display panel is clear glass and only its
  middle shows pixels. The window is the active area plus the 0.5 mm margin, read off the panel's
  centre and the spec size (`--window --no-refine`), never the whole panel: painting the panel black
  would show a black frame the phone never had. The rest of the panel stays as rendered.
- Labels that came back wrong from two edits in a row (the voicemail symbol, 'a/A' and the silent
  symbol, the activity-menu icon) were cleared to the key colour and drawn by hand
  (`text_fixes` "patch" entries); a word fix that left ghosts of the old letters was redrawn the same
  way, in Bahnschrift SemiBold at the size of the neighbouring labels.
- KLIF System CGI is shared: another job had switched it to a different model. `tools/backend.py`
  now refuses to render unless CGI runs preset `qwen-image-21`.

## What the Aino, Yari, M600i and F series taught (2026-11-11)

- GSMArena's official images (`fdn2.gsmarena.com/vv/pics/sonyericsson/se-<model>-01.jpg`) often
  show the phone open and closed side by side, straight-on: the open length is measured against the
  closed one (Aino, Yari).
- The pass 1 ranking can reject every seed of a silver body on a white background (F500i: eight
  seeds at -4.5% to -7.4%, the soft edge counts as background). The cutout of the best-looking seed
  measured -2.3%: cut it out and measure the cutout before rendering more.
- The Q of PQRS came out as an O on four of six phones; a short tail drawn by hand fixes it. The same
  small symbols keep coming back wrong (voicemail, 'a/A', the silent note, the space symbol); they
  were drawn by hand in the label colour (`text_fixes` "patch").
- The automatic window missed on five of six phones (it took the glass panel or the print). The
  windows were read off luminance profiles across each side of the screen: the edge of the content
  where the brand is printed on the glass (Yari, Jalou), the whole dark panel where nothing is printed
  on it (Aino).
- The F500i is sold with the vodafone print (Koks): an operator print that the phone came with is
  part of the phone; stickers and covers added later are not.

## What the R and G series taught (2026-11-11)

- The Sony Ericsson V series (V600i, V630i, V640i, V800) is skipped on Koks's decision: Vodafone colour
  versions of phones already in the archive.
- When the press render's gloss hides part of the brand print (G502: every render hides the 'ri' of
  Ericsson), the print is inpainted away and the same logotype from another phone in the archive
  (G900, white on dark glass) is put in its place, scaled to the print's box.
- Word fixes need boxes read off a grid of `final.png` itself: three boxes read off a 2x zoom with the
  wrong origin landed beside the words and smeared them; `pass2 --reuse` started those phones over.
- The model kept drawing the same small symbols wrong on every phone of the batch (voicemail, the
  key lock, the silent note, 'a/A'); they were drawn by hand, as were the Q of PQRS, the K of JKL and
  the X of WXYZ (Bahnschrift SemiBold SemiCondensed at the labels' cap height).
- Where the screen content runs up to the brand print (G700), give the window from the top of the
  content (the status bar), or a strip of it stays above the hole.

## What the first J batch taught (2026-11-11)

- A white phone on a white background (the white J200i) rendered 6-13% narrow on all eight seeds, and
  the cutout confirmed it: the edge melts into the background. A press render of the same model in a
  dark colour (navy J200i) gave seeds within 2%.
- An embossed brand print (J300i, the print pressed into the pink body) that comes out misspelled is
  fixed by copying whole letters from the same print ('s' of Ericsson, 'o' of Sony): a word fix
  leaves the edges of its patch on a textured surface.
- A keypad redraw can stack a symbol on another key (the * on the a of 'a/A', J210i): put that key
  back from the cutout with `restore --box` and fix its letters by hand.

## What the last J batch taught (2026-11-11)

- Read every label position off `final.png` itself (a script that lists the text components of each
  key row), not off a zoomed grid: boxes read off a zoom with the wrong origin landed beside the
  labels.
- The i of Ericsson came out as a tall l on six of seven phones; check the brand print on every
  phone, not only where a batch's first phones showed it.
- Every frontal press render of the Elm has the earphones lying over the keypad; a CC BY photo from
  Wikimedia Commons gave a clean front.
- Where the screen content ends in soft-key labels (J105i), a profile through the middle column can
  stop on a letter; read the window's bottom edge between the words.
